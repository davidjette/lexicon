**Unique Artifact · Necromantic resonance instrument · Ancient elvish fabrication · In Imperial keeping · Whereabouts withheld from every schedule**

*Also known as:* Fantanya Nyel · Fantanya Nyel · Fantanya (Hells) Nyel · Hell's Bell · Hell's Bells · the Original · the mother bell · the Greenbluff Bell · file HB-CHORUS-13

The Hell's Bell, or Fantanya Nyel, is a unique necromantic resonance instrument of ancient elvish fabrication, fused from an infernal metal, whose tones wake the dead and drive the living mad. A Celestial Queen buried it centuries ago near the Greenbluff Mines; it was dug out of those mines by the party that became [the Unforeseen](/organizations/the-unforeseen/) and carried to the Empire by train. It has been in Imperial keeping since, its whereabouts withheld from every schedule, and [Uriel Qualanthri](/people/uriel-qualanthri/) has since had hundreds of copies cast from it and hung in the Houses of Mercy across Khorvaire. Ten of the great castings remain unbroken.

## The legend

The legend of the Fantanya (Hells) Nyel (Bell) is that a Celestial Queen buried the bell centuries ago. The craftsman who made the bell were ancient elves who used an infernal metal to fuse the bell. The bell emitted horrific tones that woke the dead; those who were close to the bell and heard the ringing went “mad.” The relic is thought to be buried somewhere near the Greenbluff Mines.

The Directorate of Relic Intelligence assesses the Celestial Queen to have been **Erandis Vol** herself; others hold that she was some other power of the elvish line. [Locke Pierce](/people/locke-pierce/), who stood in the room with it, is blunter about the part that matters: the Queen did not destroy it, and he has come to believe she could not.

## Greenbluff

In recent times Crimson Sun Inquisitor [Esther Crona](/people/esther-crona/), Black Dove [Uriel Qualanthri](/people/uriel-qualanthri/), Magistrate [John C. LeBeefe](/people/john-c-lebeefe/) and an Ancient Relic Specialist from the Rekkenmark named [Locke Pierce](/people/locke-pierce/) traveled to the Greenbluff mines, as it was believed that an incident there may relate to the Hell's Bell. The Empire sent them there after an incident took place in the mine taking the lives of a number of Empire owned slaves. Survivors of the incident reported to have gone “mad” but they have described a sound deep within the mines that shook the walls so violently it caused a cave-in.

Every survivor went mad in the same shape. Beneath the risen dead, and beneath a rebel column that had come for the prize first, the party uncovered a buried chamber that had held the thing in silence for centuries.

The group retrieved the Hell's Bell from the Greenbluff mines and transported it via train back to the empire. While on the train they were attacked by rebel forces who too were after the bell.

The rebels were Fulcrum. They failed. [John Stamos](/people/john-stamos/) died on that train, and the Empire published the killing as the work of [Lady Glow](/people/lady-glow/) — a lie in its particulars and the truth in general, since both of them were rebels. Pierce met [Richard Blaze](/people/richard-blaze/) the same day, and believes it was also the day Blaze and Crona first met.

The Hell's Bell is currently in the hands of the Empire.

## The chorus

The original was never meant to hang alone. Since her elevation to caretaker Empress, [Uriel Qualanthri](/people/uriel-qualanthri/) has spent the personal wealth of the Emperor and the House of Kaius on reproductions, cast at the Korranberg Foundry Trust under standing draft and consecrated on delivery by the sisters of [the Black Doves](/organizations/the-black-doves/) alone. One burned leaf of the Trust's private schedule survived the Sharn seizure, and it carries the accounting:

|  |  |
|---|---|
| **Great castings, consecration grade** | Thirteen commissioned · eleven delivered · two in the yard |
| **Minor castings, parish grade** | Three hundred forty-one delivered to the Houses to date |
| **Small consignments** | Kept separately by the Sisters, and not by the foundry office |
| **The Original** | “Not of this foundry, and the Trust will thank the House to record that it never was.” |

The Trust received the Original under guard, for the taking of tuning references only — its masters worked in wax and never with hammers, per the terms — and returned it under escort to **Her Radiance's keeping**, destination withheld from the schedule, escort **Sister-Inquisitor M. D.** under the dove seal. The Trust then petitioned that no further reference work be sent to the yard: *the men will not stay in the shop with it after dark, and the Trust has stopped fining them for it.*

[Locke Pierce](/people/locke-pierce/)'s taxonomy divides the network into **Masters** — cathedral-grade great castings — and the parish castings beneath them. He does not believe the count stops at thirteen. *“This means there can be many many more than thirteen. I suspect there are hundreds.”*

## The Houses of Mercy

The delivery system is a charity. Sovereign Host churches, temples and hospitals were impounded by Imperial decree, their diocesan assets seized, and reopened as Houses of Mercy staffed by Black Dove nurses and nuns — a continent-scale medical network doubling as a deployment grid. The tells are consistent enough that Fulcrum built a file out of them over a decade of counting: the bell is enormous, the bell is washed at dawn like a horse, the bell has no rope, and **the bell never rings**. At Atur they did not hang it in the tower; they scaffolded it standing on the ground and raised the stone up over it. At Vathirond the graves nearest the mercy-house do not settle — the ground stays loose. The first of them all went up at the old frontier hospital in the Talenta Plains, *“the Empress's own hospital from before she was Empress, when she was just the elf who came when the plague did.”*

The file's own cover letter states the rule: *“They never ring. Not for mass, not for fire or the dead. Whatever these are, they're waiting for something and the nuns tend to them like they know what.”*

## The Black Noise

The audible toll is incidental. What the Fantanya Nyel actually emits is [the Black Noise](/lore/the-black-noise/): a polyphase arcane emission occupying multiple bands across a thousand octaves, within, above and below hearing, propagating through the arcane substrate rather than through air. It travels continental distances, penetrates hundreds of feet of rock, and is not stopped by silence. Sustained proximity corrodes the living along the Greenbluff pattern. The dead inside it do not rise wild; they rise listening.

## The Litany

Recited at the veiling of a novice, under seal of initiation. NEST agent **Foxtale** — [Gemma Corso](/people/gemma-corso/) — took it verbatim from Sister Maelis Dorn's own mouth on her ninth day inside the Korth cloister.

> come the night / the bells will toll / thirteen / the dead will rise / to serve the living / and the dragon / will be reborn

<small>“The novices treat it as poetry. Dorn does not. She counts the ‘thirteen’ on her fingers as she says it — an accounting habit, not a flourish. To her it is a checklist.” — Foxtale, NEST debrief K-KORTH</small>

## The Thirteenth Bell

Years later the bell-lore surfaced again beneath the Mror Holds, at the dwarven hold the Teryaki kin call Tempurahold, where the Korranberg Foundry had been turned to Imperial work. Descending the Grand Stair, the Inevitables found the metal named at last — Shavat , "Black Khyber," formed where Shavarath touches the world — and at the heart of an aberrant bloom a dead Imperial courier carrying a rejected Ministry requisition and a note in John C. LeBeefe's hand: "observe the deep workings and report what listens back." Beside him lay a burst glass terrarium. Its ask was plain: "Ring it here... when HE is born, it will be too late."

Across a removed bridge at the Bladeworks stood the thing itself — an enormous black bell on scaffolding, over a train cart. [The Thirteenth Casting](/items/the-thirteenth-casting/) : tuned, but never consecrated.

## King Corn and Sedda

Coded documents in the reclaimed halls told the rest. King Corn and his daughter Sedda Tiddes Deeptempura were enslaved in their own hold and planning an uprising. They were discovered. Sedda was taken below to the cavern of the black dragon Valtrex — but not before she rang the bell, three years ago. Found afterward in the shallows below, decayed but lucid, she confessed it, asked to be ended, and was given last rites and consecrated by her kin.

<small>She rang it with a rock, not with the proper hammer, on the instruction of a whispering thing in the Glitterhame she called the Speaker. She then went down to the water to wait for an extraction that was never coming, and waited three years.</small>

## The answer to the bell

King Corn had built one. His secret study held sheet music and plans for a handheld horn made to answer the bell, and beneath the study floor the prototype survived: coiled brass, unornamented, two mouthpieces, carrying his smith-mark. The activating piece is called the fanfare of dawn — played on a horn of Corn's design, a bard with enough talent can drown out the bells. His lead-bound folio holds the bell castings, the tempering and the harmonic mathematics, and on its last page: "I made their key. Someone must learn to make its answer sing."

The kin named the other answer KNELL — King Corn's black-Khyber warhammer, older than the bells, which can crack them.

**The three answers, and their limits:**

- [The Horn of Mourning](/items/the-horn-of-mourning/) — masks the Noise only while it is being played.
- [KNELL](/items/knell/) — breaks a bell permanently, and is very likely the only such hammer in the world.
- [Corn's folio](/items/corn-s-folio/) — the mathematics that make more horns possible.

## The consecration rite

Recovered from Sister Calvane's library: thirteen Doves and all thirteen planes, the bells bound to "the unholy blood and the great mother," and a very dear sacrifice — a living child, or virgin royalty. A door in those same halls had been sealed by a Black Dove prayer ritual signed by Sister Maelis Dorn .

## The Thirteenth Casting rings, and breaks

It rang. Hetta, a Teryaki scout with mould across his face and mushrooms at his lips, reached the Bladeworks past a locked door and an alarm and cast *shatter* at the casting, grazing it. The toll took the party, the camp in the next hall and the risen dead climbing the shaft into a single mind — a thirst for blood and flesh, and an urge to tear one's own skin off. Everyone inside it wounded themselves.

[Sir Dario Argentino](/people/sir-dario-argentino/) came out of it first, raised [KNELL](/items/knell/), and broke the Thirteenth Casting. The recoil deafened him and the runesmith Bofric for hours. He then walked bell to bell through the Bladeworks and destroyed every remaining casting standing in it, and the freed dragon Valtrex destroyed the original molds. Nothing is cast from that mountain again.

## Ten bells remain

Back in Sharn, Dario destroyed two more in a single morning. The first sat crated and packed with straw in the unfinished tower above the Black Dove hospital, muffled by people too frightened to touch it; he cleared the packing himself and swung with *“may our children forgive us.”* The second hung in a small temple of Boldrei in the lower city, where the strike brought the whole tower down through the floor into the building below and turned a pacified crowd against him. Research in the same week established three things: the method for making more horns, that **breaking a bell sends no word of itself back to Uriel**, and that KNELL is very likely unique.

**Ten bells remain**, at least one of them a Master in Korth — **great casting the ninth**, hanging in a Black Dove tower attached to the Imperial Palace itself. The Original was returned from the Korranberg yard to Her Radiance's keeping under Sister-Inquisitor escort, destination withheld, and the House's factor has written that the reference *will not travel again before the appointed night.*

> Stop Uriel before she rings the bells. If you fail, I will end this world before she can, and only my kind will survive it.

<small>— Locke Pierce, from Mt. Silicon, having just detonated Ruby Nova on the horizon as a demonstration.</small>

**Held by the Inevitables:** KNELL, the horn, the folio, and one dwarf who reads Corn's shorthand and can play the fanfare.

**Held by the Empire:** the Original, the ten remaining bells, three hundred and forty-one parish castings on the Trust's last count, and the woman who intends to ring them.
