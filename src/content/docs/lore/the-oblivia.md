The **Oblivia** — the **Forgetting** — is the erasure of knowledge not from a record but from *memory itself*, "from the minds of everyone who ever lived." What it touches leaves records "scarce and often cursed," and traces such as a lullaby a mother sings "as if it called to her from some forgotten recess of her mind."

## How it works

Oblivia is produced by the [Obliviator](/items/the-obliviator/), the engine above [Motherstone](/places/motherstone/): "any information that passed within its arcane horizon would be destroyed, not just in that instance, but in the minds of everyone who ever lived." It is believed to be the cause of "the forgetting of Motherstone and the dwarf king that built it" — a city that "went from the capital of the world to entirely forgotten in a day" — the event called **the Betrayal**, planned by **Calix**. The erasure itself was [Stone](/people/stone-infanta-of-discovery/)'s: the Brightmantle Infanta threw himself into the Obliviator so that his wife [Elistrada](/people/elistrae/) would forget him, and that made everything about Motherstone cursed and obliviated knowledge.

Mara Mageblood's journal names it inside a lullaby:

> When all the world's inside your mind, forgetting is oblivion,\
> so never sing this song again of — Calix the Betrayer.

## How to detect it

An Oblivia is detected by the shape of what is left rather than by anything remembered: a cursed tome whose reading costs an eye; a mind the size of a city that is broken because one date is missing from it; a sword that describes the age as "empty spaces spoiling my persistent memory."

## Time is the Obliviator

<small>At the end of time the goddess June states that the gods "created Time itself, to hide those things from him that happen not all at once… **Time is the Obliviator.**" — "him" being her father, the [Leviathan](/lore/the-leviathan/). Consciousness, a self that exists in sequence and forgets as it goes, is by her account the one place the blind devouring god cannot see.</small>

## The last Obliviation

The finale's Obliviation did not remove only the god. It removed the Infantaverse quest itself from memory, and with it the premise and existence of time travel using the [Anachron](/items/the-anachron/). The [Lexicon](/items/the-lexicon/), housed in the Anachron since the Second Battle of Motherstone, lost knowledge of its own latent power over time in the same act and did not recover it for millennia. <small>Recovered from the Oblivia. See [The Anachron](/items/the-anachron/).</small>

## Netheril's Obliviation of the orcish artificers

Oblivia was also used as an instrument of conquest. The Netherese destroyed the orcish artificers who kept [the Chronicle](/items/the-chronicle/) and obliviated their history and their historians in an attempt to reach it, and never got in. [Zebulon the Meek](/people/zebulon/), the last of those archivists, states the policy — "Those that withheld the engines of their progress from their control were to be obliviated" — and the result: "besides these paltry notes and a few specks of memory that haunt me in my sleep, I have no record or evidence that Curtis Washington ever existed… There are ruins at Yog, Jhamdaath, Enkidu, but no one alive remembers how they were built of by whom." <small>(Sic on "built of by whom." Arc VI Part IX, "The Chronicle.")</small>

## The End of the Infanta

The chronicle ends with the End of the Infanta: the heroes touch the waking god with an Obliviator, and the Wish that unwrites him unwrites them too — "they themselves will be forgotten in all but the vaguest terms. A symbol, image, relic or myth may stay behind." <small>Recovered from the Oblivia.</small>
