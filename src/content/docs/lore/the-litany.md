**Liturgical recitation · The Black Doves · Spoken under seal of initiation at the veiling of a novice**

The Litany is a liturgical recitation of the Black Doves: seven lines spoken under seal of initiation at the veiling of a novice. [Gemma Corso](/people/gemma-corso/), undercover in the Korth house, took it verbatim from [Sister Maelis Dorn](/people/sister-maelis-dorn/) and carried it out, and it is the Inevitables' evidence that the bell network runs to a schedule. The novices of the house treat it as devotional poetry; Dorn counts its "thirteen" on her fingers, and the Korranberg Foundry Trust's private schedule of works matches the count — thirteen great castings, eleven delivered and two in the yard.

> come the night\
> the bells will toll\
> thirteen\
> the dead will rise\
> to serve the living\
> and the dragon\
> will be reborn

## HOW IT WAS OBTAINED

Two accounts survive, both from the same source. In one, [Gemma Corso](/people/gemma-corso/) was deep undercover in Korth disguised as a high-elf acolyte; she befriended [Sister Maelis Dorn](/people/sister-maelis-dorn/), who believed her a worthy initiate and whispered her the forbidden poem. In the other, entered as a lay penitent of the [Host](/organizations/the-sovereign-host/) seeking work in the mercy-kitchens, Gemma stood in the choir of witnesses at a veiling on her ninth day inside the house, *with my face working correctly*, and took it verbatim from Dorn's own mouth as Dorn led the rite.

It is recited under **seal of initiation**. It is not preached, not printed, and not sung at the public offices.

## WHAT THE NOVICES THINK IT IS

Poetry. A devotional verse about the mercy of the order and the night the world is healed — the sort of thing a young sister repeats to herself in the dark and finds comforting. The novices of the Korth house treat it exactly this way.

## WHAT DORN THINKS IT IS

**An inventory.** Foxtale's assessment: *"Dorn does not. She counts the 'thirteen' on her fingers as she says it — an accounting habit, not a flourish. To her it is a checklist."*

> "The novices treat it as poetry. Dorn does not." — Agent FOXTALE, NEST debrief K-KORTH

The Korranberg Foundry Trust's private schedule of works confirms what the fingers are counting: **great castings, consecration grade — thirteen commissioned, eleven delivered, two in the yard**, with three hundred and forty-one parish castings beneath them and the small consignments kept by the Sisters and not by the foundry.

<small>"Thirteen" counts two different things in the Doves' liturgy and both are correct. Thirteen **Master bells**, the great castings. And thirteen **Doves** and all thirteen **planes** in the consecration rite recovered from [Sister Calvane](/people/sister-calvane/)'s library, which binds a bell to "the unholy blood and the great mother" and requires a very dear sacrifice — a living child, or virgin royalty. The 341 parish castings sit beneath the thirteen Masters and are not counted in the Litany at all.</small>

## LINE BY LINE, AGAINST THE RECORD

- **come the night** — the sisterhood's own name for the operation is the **Eternal Nightfall**, given up by [Sister Nora](/people/sister-nora/) under interrogation at Zilspar and whispered once by her at POSÉ.
- **the bells will toll** — the [Fantanya Nyel](/items/the-hell-s-bell-fantanya-nyel/) castings hanging in the [Houses of Mercy](/organizations/the-houses-of-mercy/), washed daily, never rung.
- **thirteen** — the great castings. Eleven delivered and two in the yard when the leaf was written; the Thirteenth was broken with KNELL under the Korramont and two more in Sharn; ten bells remain, at least one of them a Master in Korth.
- **the dead will rise** — the Black Noise raises the dead where they lie, through rock, at continental distance.
- **to serve the living** — *"ready to serve the will of a sufficiently powerful necromancer keyed in to the chorus. There is exactly one necromancer alive with the reach the network is tuned for."*
- **and the dragon** — unresolved. Every candidate in the archive is a different answer: [Viagra](/people/viagra/), the great dragon of Master Armhair's heresy; the Crimson Sun, who fathered [Erandis Vol](/people/erandis-vol/)'s child; Valtrex, bled for three years; or a child not yet born.
- **will be reborn** — the network is not set to a calendar.

## THE INEVITABLES' READING

Assembled by [Gemma](/people/gemma-corso/), [Eric](/people/eric-the-cleric/) and [Dario](/people/sir-dario-argentino/) in a borrowed apartment in Korth, from three sources. **It is their inference, not Black Dove doctrine.**

- [Uriel Qualanthri](/people/uriel-qualanthri/) bears the [Mark of Death](/lore/the-mark-of-death-and-the-dragonmarks/) and descends from [House Vol](/organizations/house-vol/).
- Erandis Vol, her ancestor, bore a child by a dragon called the Crimson Sun — Valtrex's testimony, under the Korramont.
- Master Armhair's recovered manuscript threads the Mark of Death, Erandis Vol's birth, and one dragon together.
- The Litany, recited under seal at every veiling, ends *and the dragon will be reborn*.
- **Therefore: Uriel is trying to bear a dragon-sired son.**
- Which accounts for the **fourteen daughters** she has by Emperor Kaius, many sets of twins, all under twelve years old — and for **three years of draining Valtrex** on a rig built to harpoon, restrain and bleed him, with the blood shipped out by rail.

The operational consequence, which the party reached in Korth Ep 4: **Uriel is waiting for a specific event, possibly the birth of a male heir.**

<small>The theory leaves two things open. Valtrex believes the heirs are already his, which would mean the dragon-siring has been happening for years and has produced fourteen girls. And the Emperor's own nature is disputed — Armhair says dragon, LeBeefe's voice says vampire descended from the Crimson Sun. If the Emperor is himself the dragon, the blood on the rail was never needed. Neither reading is proven.</small>

## The Answer

King Corn of the Deeptempura spent his last days as a slave perfecting a melody. Bofric the runesmith translated it out of the folio and identified it as arcane harmonics — **an elvish weapon from the old war against the dragons**. Played on Corn's brass horn with the fanfare of dawn, it suppresses a bell's influence entirely, though only while it plays. At the hospital in Sharn a representative of every faith on the continent learned it and carried it home, and the clergy named it the **Horn of Mourning**.
