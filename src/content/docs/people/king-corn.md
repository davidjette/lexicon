**Duergar (deep dwarf) · King of the Deeptempura · House Deeptempura · Dead**

*Also known as:* Corn Deeptempura - King Corn of the Deeptempura

**King Corn of the Deeptempura** was a duergar king and smith who ruled the [Deeptempura](/organizations/the-deeptempura-clan/) forge-clan and the whole interior of the [Korramont](/places/the-korramont/). He made his hold twice by treaty: an infernal pact for the art of forging **Shavat**, of which the **Crown of Seven Stars** is the token, and a peace treaty with the black dragon recorded in stone as **Vuldrach**. Roughly ten or eleven years before the present the House of Kaius bought the Korramont and put the clan to work as chattel in its own hold, casting for the House by standing draft; Corn and his daughter [Sedda Tiddes Deeptempura](/people/sedda-tiddes-deeptempura/) planned an uprising and forged a handheld horn to counteract the bells, and when the resistance was discovered a writ of warrant signed **KAIUS III** killed roughly 97% of the dwarvish workforce. Corn ended crowned on [Valtrex](/species/valtrex/)'s hoard on the island in the [black lake](/places/the-black-lake/), where his remains still lie. Three years later the dragon raised him with *speak with dead*, and the dead king named "the white queen" as the author of the massacre and told him to help break the bells.

## Physical description

No living witness has described him. What survives of King Corn is a body and a great many pictures of one. On the relief walls flanking the [Grand Stair](/places/the-grand-stair/) he is a king in a crown bearing **seven stars** - the same figure as the Warden Statue at the [Mountain Door](/places/the-mountain-door/), and the same crown that turns up three years later on a corpse at the top of a dragon's hoard. In the panel where a seven-fingered devil hands him that crown his hands are drawn wearing symbolic golden shackles, and they are drawn that way in every panel after it. The corpse itself lies crowned on the island in the [black lake](/places/the-black-lake/), folded around a warhammer, in acid air.

## The clan and the hold

The [Deeptempura](/organizations/the-deeptempura-clan/) were a deep-dwarf forge-clan holding the whole interior of the [Korramont](/places/the-korramont/), from the fortified [Mountain Door](/places/the-mountain-door/) at the surface down through the sepulchers, the [Glitterhame](/places/the-glitterhame/) and the [Great Hall of Rak'tafur](/places/the-great-hall-of-rak-tafur/) to the lake beneath. Corn ruled it as king and worked in it as a smith; his personal mark is stamped on objects the surviving kin still recognise on sight. The stair reliefs record his reign as the clan wanted it remembered: the descent into the ore, the bargain, the treaty, the works.

## Public and private

In the Empire's paperwork he is not a king at all. The [Korranberg Foundry Trust](/organizations/the-korranberg-foundry-trust/) keeps archaic property records of dwarvish slaves it owns and a personnel ledger that counts heads, and the death warrant that ends him opens with the name **Corn Deeptempura** and no title. To his own people he remained king the entire time he was a slave in his own hold.

In private, at the end, he was doing arithmetic. His study held sheet music, treasure and drawings of horns worked against harmonic-resonance mathematics - a king teaching himself wave symmetry in secret, under guard, while his own foundry cast the instruments of a continental massacre.

## Two bargains

Corn made his hold twice, and both times by treaty.

**The devil.** The left-hand relief shows him leading a crowd of dwarves into a mountain of polished black ore - the discovery of the Glitterhame, named in stone as *a cavern full of infernal ore* - and ends with the king before an enormous winged, horned devil that points at an anvil. It is the formal record of an infernal pact for the art of forging **Shavat**. The devil's name is written nowhere in the mountain. [Valtrex](/species/valtrex/) later put a number on the deal: *"Corn made seven pledges to that devil."* The **Crown of Seven Stars** is the token of them, and merely touching it reveals the bearer's location to its maker.

**The dragon.** The right-hand relief shows dwarves at anvils bearing swords and axes, and ends with a drake coiled round an anvil and the king before it with his arms open: a peace treaty with the black dragon recorded in stone as **Vuldrach**. Further along the same hallway, Corn brings the dragon tribute. Valtrex holds that the duergar broke the pact by letting the Empire into his lair.

## Competence and circle

His mother was [Queen Pu-Pu Platter Deeptempura](/people/queen-pu-pu-platter-deeptempura/), whose sarcophagus in the sepulcher gallery has never been opened. His daughter and heir was [Sedda Tiddes Deeptempura](/people/sedda-tiddes-deeptempura/). The clan's runesmith [Bofric](/people/bofric/) still reads his shorthand, and it was Bofric who recovered the one thing Corn most wanted read.

**Items:**

- **KNELL** - his personal black-Khyber warhammer, older than the bells, named for the party by the kin. It cracks anything made of Shavat. Recovered from his corpse's arms on the hoard; carried now by [Sir Dario Argentino](/people/sir-dario-argentino/).
- **The lead-bound folio** - the complete record of the forging: bell castings, tempering and hammering methods, harmonic-frequency mathematics, horn designs. It is a *Tome of Understanding*. Sealed in a chest under his study floor and opened for the first time since the massacre.
- **The horn** - coiled brass, unornamented, asymmetrical, two mouthpieces, his smith-mark on it. Brass, which means it can be copied.
- **The Crown of Seven Stars** - infernal work, cursed. Claimed by Valtrex as his own property, traded away, and now in [Gemma Corso's](/people/gemma-corso/) bag, unworn.
- **Corn-made weapons** - many recovered in the hold, all +1 adamantine and stamped with his master seal. <small>(Oral Histories: The Inevitables, 2026-07-29)</small>

## The Answering Note

Corn spent his final days enraptured with a muse, perfecting a song: twenty-four bars with a short eight-bar bridge, in a very dwarven style. It is not decoration. Played on a horn built to his design it drowns the bells out entirely. Bofric names the discipline **arcane harmonics** - procured by the elves as a weapon in the old war against the dragons, and only ever mastered by the dwarves. The clan calls the piece **the fanfare of dawn**. Its limit is exactly what Bofric says it is: *"It can only maybe drown out the sound as long as you play it. To break the bell, you would need to break it with the hammer that made it."*

On the folio's last page, in plain Common, in a shaking hand:

> I made their key. Someone must learn to make its answer sing.

Beside a drawing of a great hammer striking a bell, one legible margin note: **wounding**.

## Beliefs

He believed a civilisation is an alloy - his folio is a treatise on metallurgy and leadership that argues the two are one craft.

He believed a thing he had made could be unmade by someone he would never meet, and he addressed his last page to that person rather than to his own people.

## Origin

The [Korranberg Foundry](/places/the-korranberg-foundry/) was Deeptempura work long before it was Imperial. Then the House of Kaius bought the Korramont, roughly ten or eleven years ago, rebuilt the freight rail into the King's Forest, and put the clan to work as chattel in its own hold. For about seven years the foundry cast for the House by standing draft - ore in, "large classified objects" out, on a line that officially never existed.

Corn and his daughter, both slaves, communicated in encoded messages and planned an uprising. In secret they forged a handheld horn meant to counteract or resist the bells. The resistance was discovered. Sedda was taken below into the dragon's water; Corn ended crowned on the dragon's hoard. The instrument of it survives: a **writ of warrant for the death** of a long list of names, opening with **Corn Deeptempura** and **Sedda Tiddes Deeptempura**, including the [Black Dove](/organizations/the-black-doves/) Sister Calvane, and ending with the foundry's own [Overseer Velen Marsk](/people/overseer-velen-marsk/), who signed to receive it. Kill everyone, then kill yourself. It is signed **KAIUS III**. The Trust's own unofficial tally puts the dead at roughly 97% of the dwarvish workforce, in what the paperwork calls "the accident."

## The ghost

In the foundry in [Korth Ep 8](/sessions/korth-ep-8-the-thirteenth-bell/) the Inevitables met Corn's ghost. Dave described it as "the ghost of a guy with a hammer in chains". It lamented that his daughter Sedda had been taken downstairs, begged the party to find her, and promised that if they did it would tell them everything and lead them to his Folio. Afterward the party did not tell [General Tso](/people/general-tso/) that they had seen the ghost of Corn. <small>(Oral Histories: The Inevitables, 2026-07-26)</small>

## Speak with dead

Three years on, freed of the bell chained around his own neck, Valtrex leaned over the crowned corpse at the heart of his hoard and called it up:

> Dwarf king, Corn, arise. Tell me what has befallen in this place. What has happened to us? How was I come to be bewitched? My blood drawn, my treasure stolen. Why have you died?

The answer named the author of it, and it was not the Emperor whose signature is on the warrant:

> The white queen. The queen of death. She is the one who did this to you, to us. All is lost.

Asked how she could be destroyed, the dead king gave the dragon an instruction that has since been carried out on the Thirteenth Casting and on every small casting in the [Bladeworks](/places/the-bladeworks/): **"You must help them break the bells."**

Because the party had helped his ghost, Corn gave them his blessing when he was raised. Freeing Valtrex had turned the dragon from hostile to indifferent; persuasion, the trade and "the endorsement of Corns ghost" <small>(sic)</small> then let him part with the hammer. <small>(Oral Histories: The Inevitables, 2026-08-22)</small>

## Right now

King Corn's remains are still on the island in the black lake, under a dragon who considers the crown and the hammer his own property and has forbidden anyone to return. The kin's errand is half-finished - his daughter was brought home and consecrated; the king was not, and the clan was never even told where he lies. His key is broken. His answer is loose in the world, in a brass horn, a lead-bound folio, and the head of one runesmith.
