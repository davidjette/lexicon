**Duergar (deep dwarf) · Queen of the Deeptempura · House Deeptempura · Dead**

Queen Pu-Pu Platter Deeptempura was queen of the [Deeptempura](/organizations/the-deeptempura-clan/) and the mother of [King Corn](/people/king-corn/), the clan's last king. She is buried in a royal sarcophagus at the heart of the masoned sepulcher gallery above the [Glitterhame](/places/the-glitterhame/), on consecrated ground [the bloom](/species/the-bloom/) cannot cross. The **Inevitables** robbed the gallery of six dwarven ceremonial belts but could not open her sarcophagus. Her reign, her consort, her deeds and the manner of her death are not recorded anywhere in the mountain that has been read; almost everything known about her is on the sarcophagus itself.

## The sepulcher gallery

She lies in the masoned gallery above the Glitterhame - everlasting flames, luminescent crystal, and consecrated ground that is the one surface in the cavern the bloom cannot cross. Her royal sarcophagus sits at the heart of it, and every other casket in the gallery holds an undead sealed inside, trapped since the night the bell rang three years ago.

The Inevitables tried to open hers. Strength failed, *plant growth* failed, and fire failed. It stayed shut, held by something beyond its own weight.

## What was taken

The gallery was robbed anyway. Two living wreaths held six dwarven ceremonial belts, and the party took five jewelled ones and a **Belt of Dwarvenkind** - the latter turning out to belong to the late husband of [Madame Tso Teryaki](/people/madame-tso-teryaki/), who identified it across a dark hall and named the party grave robbers to their faces.

The Belt of Dwarvenkind was surrendered, confiscated, and much later returned. The five jewelled belts were never given back.

## Right now

Sealed, undisturbed, and on consecrated ground in a mountain that now belongs to a black dragon.

**Organizations / Groups:** House Deeptempura, by birth or marriage; the record does not say which.

**Holdings:** The royal sarcophagus, sepulcher gallery, the Korramont.
