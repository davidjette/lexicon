**City · Kingdom of Karrnath · Political capital and ceremonial seat of the Pax Imperium · Standing**

*Also known as:* The Imperial Capital · the Capitol

Korth is the political capital of the Kingdom of Karrnath and the ceremonial seat of the Empire. It is old, walled, dense, and vertical in the European way rather than the impossible way [Sharn](/places/sharn/) is vertical. It is not the Empire's largest city or its administrative brain, and much of it is ceremonial space that stands empty most of the year.

It is also a garrison. The army lives inside the walls, the Empire's last airships and death troopers ring the outside, and columns of soldiers — living and undead both — move to and from the barracks all night.

The city is clean, quiet, tasteful and morbid. Korth has none of Sharn's industrial grime, and the steam system is spotless and egalitarian.

## Steam and aqua vitae

Korth runs on steam. It hisses and bubbles under everything, powering lights, drainage pumps, plumbing, heating and every otherwise-magical contraption in the city. Huge **steam vats** stand in the open near the ministries, generating and storing pressurised steam, and open **hot pools** vent in the west — infrastructure that doubles as a quiet park where people keep their voices down.

The working fluid is **aqua vitae**: a salty, faintly proteinous liquid, less compressible than water and therefore prized for hydraulics. It is kept entirely separate from the drinking supply.

A decommissioned lightning rail subway runs beneath the city. The station is still there; there are people alive who remember when it was open.

## The compass

The city was built out of ancient forums and plazas, so no building sits neatly inside its own footprint. Entrances are on odd sides, levels connect strangely, and service entrances and officers' entrances are separate from public ones. Visitors lose time to this.

**Center — the Great Plaza and the Parade Throne.** The ceremonial heart. A grand stair rises to a ceremonial throne where the Emperor once sat for parades; it has been a very long time. You pass underneath the throne structure to cross between the upper plaza and the lower. Everything in the centre is owned outright by the Empire, and its towers have been converted into housing you rent from the Empire or are assigned as part of your post, one shared bathroom to a floor.

**The Lower Plaza.** Below and beyond the parade stair, toward the palace: the one part of the core that belongs to ordinary people. A public administration building serves as a great rotunda and town hall where rations are distributed to Karrnathi peasants in queues under guard. The corruption is visible if you watch — guards take payment for extra rations. The Empire's statue of [John Stamos](/people/john-stamos/) stands in the city.

**North — the Ministry of Records.** A giant building that has been a library, a university and a wizard's tower at different points in a very long history. It is not a general library; it exists to hold the most controlled material — sensitive government documents, secrets, licences, records of the Emperor's ancestors, subjects of great magical knowledge. It has been emptied out and backfilled with fluff, the legal section is down to a handful of tomes, and any record naming a specific dragon has been removed by order. Scholars work and loiter here at all hours, and on the third level sits a clerk who will forge identity papers and weapon registrations for a bribe.

**Northeast — the Ministry of Law.** Next door to Records: where laws are made and negotiated and where trials are held, with a separate side entrance for officers and upper floors full of advocates. The High Court for capital cases sits on an upper level, overlooked by a balcony holding an **Iron Maiden** that kills the condemned while leaving the corpse intact for use. The jail occupies the northeast corner of the same complex, two levels of cells watched by Imperial Red Guard. Behind the building a stair descends to the dumpster yard, where a **corpse chute** empties the dead into stacks for reuse.

**South — the Great Imperial Palace and the Throne of Bones.** Far beyond the parade throne. Theoretically the seat of the Emperor's power, but he has not sat the Throne of Bones in ten years. Bureaucrats and nobles still work there through intermediaries of the court. Attached to the palace stands a tower — a [Black Dove](/organizations/the-black-doves/) cloister and convent with a bell at its top. That bell is **great casting the ninth**, a Master among the [Fantanya Nyel](/items/the-hell-s-bell-fantanya-nyel/), delivered and consecrated under the dove seal. It will be heavily guarded.

**South — the Hall of Submission.** Open to the public and busy. Where the very old go willingly to cross over and begin their "second service to the Emperor," and where the dead are brought to be transformed. Officers wait outside with troop transports to collect converts. It is a good place to talk, because almost no one in earshot is alive.

**East — the Listening Post.** Beside the Hall of Submission. An Imperial intelligence office holding, among other things, the records of how Fulcrum was rolled up. Further east stands a building forbidden to enter, ringed with the nastier grades of troops; its contents are unknown.

**Southwest — the Barracks and the Altar of the Guardian.** Across a large bridge west of the Great Plaza. The military is such a large share of the population that the barracks mess is effectively open to the public, which makes it the one reliable civic amenity and a terrible place to be a criminal. Beside it stands the Altar of the Guardian, holding the tomb of [Varyn Crona](/people/varyn-crona/) and serving as the seat of [the Crimson Sun](/organizations/the-crimson-sun/) when that order was active. It reads like a tomb of the unknown soldier: a monument locals never visit on purpose. A secret military compound stands next to it, purpose unknown.

**The river.** Passenger boats depart from the river near the city, notably the vessel to [the Summer Palace](/places/the-summer-palace/) and [the Ivory Lazaret](/places/the-ivory-lazaret/), which sails twice a day.

## Who lives here

The army proper — paladins, knights and stormtroopers of various castes — plus Imperial Guard in red robes: the same silent, eyeless, undead honour troops the Inevitables fought at [Thronehold](/places/thronehold/). They stand at attention all night. Whatever is inside the red does not sleep.

Korth was and outwardly remains a human city with big human institutions, but High Elven influence has thickened steadily since the Emperor's birth and is now heavy. The elves remain in their place: they do not rule openly, they shape. Karrnath learned its military necromancy from the elves of the Undying Court, who brought official necromancy with them.

Tieflings serve in the Imperial ranks. Leonin live here. There is at least one species so unfamiliar that a lifelong Karrnathi has never seen it and does not know what it is; the local slur for them is "Glip Glops." Korth has its own distinct accent.

There is no underworld inside the walls. Korth is a cloistered, walled military headquarters with no commerce or carousing to speak of, and thieves' cant got no response from anyone the party tried in the city. The right contact inside can point you to things *outside* the walls. That is the way in.

## Karrnathi law

You are not the defendant. You are **the condemned**. Guilt is the premise.

The system descends from the old Galifar courts and was genuinely revolutionary when it split off, creating a judicial bureaucracy notionally separate from the Emperor, its panels mythologised as virtuous paragons who have exonerated people later celebrated as heroes. It does not seek truth. It seeks **atonement**. The doctrine is a kind of original sin: you may be innocent of this particular charge, but you are guilty of something, and the only road back into society runs through submitting to the will of the court — which is a stand-in for, and an extension of, the Emperor. This is a society that effectively mandates that you give your corpse to the state.

There is no defence counsel and no discovery. A panel of judges — eight sat for Bantide Moro — interrogates the prosecution's reasoning and decides for itself. In place of a defence, a person with **standing** may bear witness to the condemned's value to society: kin, an employer, a patron, a ward. The state qualifies who holds that standing. Acquittal is a perfectly common outcome of a functioning atonement system.

Sabotage, espionage and embezzlement carry death. Falsifying records carries a term equivalent to twenty-five human years; sentences are scaled by race and expected lifespan. "Monster races" receive far harsher penalties, are denied advocates entirely, and have bespoke execution methods defined so that they can be executed at all. A death sentence specifies both the duration of pain to be inflicted during the killing and the length of service owed afterward in undeath.

Smuggling weapons into the city is punishable by death. An armed, unenlisted bodyguard in the city centre must be licensed and insured under the **Imperial Blackguards and Mercenary Act**; a weapon registration is Form 7-168.

## Right now

Three operatives of the Inevitables are living in a dead advocate's apartment on the lower plaza under her name and her paralegal's. A woman officially recorded as a suicide in custody is hiding with them. A Master bell hangs in the palace cloister, a month runs on the clock before the state must re-try Bantide Moro with better evidence, and a boat leaves twice a day for the Empress.

**Places of Interest:**

- **The Great Plaza and the Parade Throne:** the ceremonial heart, empty most of the year.
- **The Ministry of Records:** the Hall of Records, scrubbed of dragons, full of scholars.
- **The Ministry of Law:** the High Court, the Iron Maiden, the jail and the corpse chute.
- **The Great Imperial Palace:** the Throne of Bones, unsat for ten years, and a Black Dove cloister with the ninth great casting in its tower.
- **The Hall of Submission:** where the old go to begin their second service.
- **The Listening Post:** Imperial intelligence, and the file on how Fulcrum fell.
- **The Barracks:** the mess is open to the public, which is the only reason it matters.
- **The Altar of the Guardian:** the tomb of Varyn Crona and the old seat of the Crimson Sun.
- **The hot pools:** vented steam infrastructure in the west that doubles as a park.
- **The river landing:** boats twice daily to the Summer Palace and the Ivory Lazaret.
