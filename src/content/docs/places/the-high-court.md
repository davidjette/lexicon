**Landmark · Capital court of the Ministry of Law · Upper level, northeast [Korth](/places/korth/)**

*Also known as:* the panel · the court of the condemned

The High Court is the capital court of the Ministry of Law in Korth, one level above the ground floor of the Ministry building in the northeast of the city. Capital matters are heard there by a panel of eight seated judges, with the condemned in irons at the centre of them and [the Iron Maiden](/items/the-iron-maiden/) on a balcony in full view of the room. Under [Karrnathi law](/lore/karrnathi-law/) there is no defence.

## The Ministry building

The Ministry of Law stands next door to [the Hall of Records](/places/the-hall-of-records/) in the northeast of the city, with upper floors full of advocates and a separate side entrance for officers. Laws are made and negotiated there. Capital cases are heard **one level up**, reached by a set of stairs from the ground floor.

## The chamber

A **panel of seated judges** — eight for a capital matter — with the condemned in irons at the centre of them, guarded. There is no defence table, because under Karrnathi law there is no defence. The state's advocate stands, reads the charges, demonstrates and distributes the evidence, and calls for the sentence; the panel's stated function is to interrogate the prosecution's reasoning and decide for itself.

Beyond the panel, in full view of the room, a balcony holds the Iron Maiden — the device that kills the condemned while preserving the body intact for [second service](/lore/second-service/). The chamber is arranged so that the accused, the judges and the machine are all looking at each other.

The judges are mythologised as virtuous paragons who have exonerated people later celebrated as heroes of the people, and acquittal is a perfectly common outcome. The First Judge opens the matter, and proceedings begin whether or not the state's advocate has arrived.

> "Advocate Moro, I'm glad that you could come. We were just about to adjourn and pass summary judgment." — the First Judge

## The jail

The northeast corner of the same complex holds the jail: two levels of cells, prisoners in varying states of distress and undress, watched by **Imperial Red Guard** — the silent, eyeless undead who register nothing said in front of them, including a confession that the Emperor is a dragon. Behind the building, a stair descends to the dumpster yard, where a **corpse chute** empties the dead into stacks for reuse. An officer stops departing visitors to ask whether the prisoner was given any foreign object, poison, contaminant, magic item, cursed item or potential choking hazard.

> "Well, if you weren't overstuffing our prison every day, we'd have enough resources to keep them alive long enough to execute." — the jailer-officer, apologising

## The State v. Bantide Moro

On a Friday morning eight judges heard the capital charges against [Bantide Moro](/people/bantide-moro/), read by an advocate wearing the face of the condemned's own sister. Several of them registered admiration at the clarity of the delivery. The condemned threw herself on the mercy of the court and confessed to having failed the Emperor.

Then [Gemma Corso](/people/gemma-corso/) set the advocate's role down and spoke as [kin](/lore/kin-and-patron/). Vice Admiral Freyd Merkin stormed the floor, objected, and was made to concede the doctrine aloud. The panel deliberated, found the documentation **insufficient to sustain the capital claims**, granted the state one additional month to acquire evidence, and remanded the condemned to the custody of the local garrison. The Iron Maiden on the balcony went unused.
