**Established faith · Continent-wide · Dispossessed; its buildings still standing under another order's flag**

The Sovereign Host is an established, continent-wide faith of Khorvaire, holding two seats on the Imperial Council and, within a decade, dispossessed of everything else. Its dioceses and abbeys were impounded by Imperial decree and their considerable assets seized; its churches, temples and hospitals were turned into [Houses of Mercy](/organizations/the-houses-of-mercy/) staffed by [Black Dove](/organizations/the-black-doves/) nurses and nuns, with Hell's Bells hung in them. Its congregations were not dissolved with its sees, and after the fall of Sharn its clergy were among those who took up teaching the Horn of Mourning.

## THE IMPERIAL COUNCIL ROSTER

Two seats on the [Imperial Council](/organizations/the-imperial-council-imperial-assembly/) belong to the Host: **Dunkin Sawbrass**, human cleric, Pope of the Sovereign Host, and **Garth Brooks**, human cleric, Bishop of the Sovereign Host. A parenthetical query against the second name asks whether the Bishop is the secret leader of the [Blood of Vol](/organizations/blood-of-vol/) under the name Chris Gaines. No answer was ever written beneath it.

It was Garth Brooks, with Chancellor Magnolia Steel of the Rekkenmark, who sent [Esther Crona](/people/esther-crona/) to Newham on the tablet assignment — which is to say that the Host's own bishop dispatched the party that would later carry the [Fantanya Nyel](/items/the-hell-s-bell-fantanya-nyel/) out of Greenbluff.

## THE MT. SILICON DOSSIER — THE SEIZURE OF THE SEES

By Imperial decree the dioceses and abbeys of the Sovereign Host were impounded and their considerable assets seized. The churches, temples and hospitals were turned into Houses of Mercy staffed by Black Dove nurses and nuns. The dossier records the seizure in the same sentence as the bankrolling of the bells from the personal wealth of the Emperor and the House of Kaius; whether the Host's endowments paid for the castings that now hang in the Host's own cathedrals, it does not say.

**Named foundations taken:** the old Host cathedral at **Korth**, which received the ninth great casting and had its west door widened to admit it · the Grand Infirmary of the Host at **Fairhaven** · the monastery precincts at **Atur** · the mercy-house at **Wroat** · **Vathirond** · the old frontier hospital in the **Talenta Plains** · a small temple of Boldrei in the Lower City of Sharn, which held a bell until Dario broke it <small>(Boldrei is a Sovereign of the Host — published Eberron canon)</small> · the **Starilaskur** Sovereign shrine, which raised a new bell by subscription and rings it every dawn, and which is therefore *not* a House of Mercy.

## THE LAITY

The congregations were not dissolved with the sees, and the ordinary practice of the faith is the cover under which much of this intelligence was gathered. [Gemma Corso](/people/gemma-corso/) entered the Korth house *as a lay penitent of the Host seeking work in the mercy-kitchens*, and the sisters took her without question. A midwife at Wroat asked the sisters to ring for a christening *the way any chapel would* and was told the bell is consecrated and does not ring for such things.

<small>Father White, a Priest of the Sovereign Host, and his paladins fought alongside the [Unforeseen](/organizations/the-unforeseen/) on the Talenta Plains Express, in the years before any of this.</small>

## After the Fall of Sharn

When the coalition took Sharn the [Silver Flame](/organizations/the-silver-flame/) moved first and hardest: the Doves outlawed, the hospital seized, citywide cremation enforced. The Host recovered nothing formally. What it did receive was an invitation. At the hospital, [Zero of Sharn](/people/zero-of-sharn/) convened a representative of every faith on the continent; Bofric played the fanfare of the Dawn; and the clergy took up teaching the **Horn of Mourning** congregation by congregation.

**Holdings:** whatever was not impounded. No complete inventory survives on either side of the seizure.
